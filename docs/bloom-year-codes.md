# Independent codes for Central Texas bluebonnet springs, 2016–2026

These codes are the external check on the iNaturalist strength index (hypothesis H1 in `preregistration.md`). The coding rule below was committed before any code was assigned, and the person or agent assigning codes works only from published text and does not see iNaturalist counts or weather data.

## Coding rule (committed 2026-09-25, before coding)

1. **Sources, in order of preference.**
   - Statements by the Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center, Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, or Texas Department of Transportation staff.
   - Articles in *Texas Highways*, and in Austin or statewide news outlets: KXAN, KVUE, KUT, Fox 7 Austin, *Austin American-Statesman*, *Texas Tribune*, *Houston Chronicle*, *San Antonio Express-News*, CultureMap.
   - Only text published between 1 February of the spring and 31 January of the following year is eligible. Text published before 1 February is a forecast, not an observation, and is recorded but not coded.
2. **Region.** The statement must concern bluebonnets (or "wildflowers" where bluebonnets are named) in Central Texas, the Austin area, or the Hill Country. Statements about other regions only (for example the Big Bend or the Rio Grande Valley) are not used.
3. **Codes.**
   - **good (2):** the bloom is described as above average, strong, abundant, spectacular, excellent, or similar, without a qualifier restricting it to a small part of the region.
   - **poor (0):** the bloom is described as below average, sparse, thin, poor, disappointing, or reduced, or fewer bluebonnets are reported than usual.
   - **moderate (1):** the bloom is described as average, typical, mixed, patchy, or variable, or the eligible statements disagree.
   - **not coded:** no eligible statement exists for that spring.
4. **Several statements.** When a spring has several eligible statements, the code is the one supported by the majority. A tie between good and poor is coded moderate.
5. **Record.** Every statement used is recorded with its source, date, URL, and a verbatim quotation of 40 words or fewer.

## Codes

Codes assigned 2026-09-25 by an independent coder working from published text only. The coder did not query iNaturalist, GBIF, USA-NPN, or weather data.

Conventions used in applying the rule:

- One publication counts as one statement. When one article contains several relevant sentences, they are quoted together under a single bullet, and the article counts once toward the majority. Syndicated or regional copies of the same article (for example the CultureMap Austin, Houston, Dallas, and San Antonio editions) count once.
- Statements published on or after 1 February are eligible even when worded as a prediction. Such statements are marked "predictive" below.
- A statement that is conditional on weather that had not yet occurred (for example "if we get rain ... it should be") does not describe a bloom and is not counted.
- Where a single statement contains both a moderate and a good or poor descriptor, the code follows the descriptor applied to bluebonnets in Central Texas, the Austin area, or the Hill Country. If no such descriptor exists, the code follows the headline or lead sentence.
- Sources not named in rule 1 (for example CBS Austin/KEYE and *Austin Monthly*) are recorded and counted at the lowest level of preference. In no spring does the code change when they are removed.
- Where good and moderate statements are equal in number, the statements disagree, and the spring is coded moderate under rule 3.

### 2016

- Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center, "2016 Wildflower Forecast," Center staff, 1 March 2016, https://www.wildflower.org/pressroom/2016-wildflower-forecast. Predictive. "Rain finally arrived in some areas the last week of February and the places it fell, which can vary even within a city, should enjoy excellent displays. Blooming will be less spectacular where the rain hasn't been steady." Supports **moderate** (variable).
- *Austin American-Statesman*, Mauri Elbel, "10 spectacular spots for wildflowers," https://www.statesman.com/story/lifestyle/travel/2016/09/03/10-spectacular-spots-for-wildflowers/10153764007/. The page metadata gives 3 September 2016, but the text refers to the 8–10 April Burnet festival as "this weekend," so it was written in early April 2016. The date is within the eligible window in either case. "This season's bluebonnet boom is courtesy of the record rains and mild winter experienced throughout Central Texas." Also: "Coming and going from Llano is a spectacular sight this year. Roadsides are carpeted with bluebonnets and fiery Indian paintbrush all along Highway 71 in both directions." Supports **good**.
- Texas Parks and Wildlife Department news release, 22 April 2016, https://tpwd.texas.gov/newsmedia/releases/?req=20160422b. Jason Singhurst (TPWD): "Most of the state has had a great spring wildflower response … State parks and wildlife management areas, especially in the upper coast, Hill Country and north central Texas, have been extremely impressive." Also: "Neffendorf said LBJ has seen an abundance of Texas bluebonnets along the park trails." Supports **good**.
- Not counted (source not in rule 1 and not a news outlet): Gary Regner, Texas wildflower sightings report, March 2016, http://www.texaswildflowerpictures.com/texas-wildflower-report-2016.htm. "Looks like 2016 is a dud." This would support poor. It is recorded because it disagrees with the counted statements.
